## 1. States of Matter & Phase Transitions

### 1.1 The Five States

| State | Description | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid** | Fixed shape and volume; particles in tight lattice | Rigid structure |
| **Liquid** | Fixed volume, no fixed shape; particles flow | Fluid but incompressible |
| **Gas** | No fixed shape or volume; particles far apart | Compressible, fills container |
| **Plasma** | Ionized gas; electrons stripped from nuclei | Electrically conductive; found in stars, lightning, flames |
| **Bose-Einstein Condensate** | Ultra-cold; atoms collapse to ground state | Lab curiosity; not practical for game |

> **Game note:** Plasma is achievable via extreme heat (>10,000 °C) or electrical discharge. Lightning naturally creates plasma channels.

### 1.2 Phase Transition Names

| Transition | From → To | Energy Change | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melting** (Fusion) | Solid → Liquid | Absorbs heat | Ice → Water at 0 °C |
| **Freezing** (Solidification) | Liquid → Solid | Releases heat | Water → Ice at 0 °C |
| **Vaporization** (Boiling/Evaporation) | Liquid → Gas | Absorbs heat | Water → Steam at 100 °C |
| **Condensation** | Gas → Liquid | Releases heat | Steam → Water droplets |
| **Sublimation** | Solid → Gas (skips liquid) | Absorbs heat | Dry ice (CO₂) at −78.5 °C |
| **Deposition** | Gas → Solid (skips liquid) | Releases heat | Water vapor → Frost on grass |

*Source: [NOAA NESDIS Water Cycle](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2025-08/WaterCycleActivity-Manual.pdf); [NOAA GML Critical Thinking Activity](https://gml.noaa.gov/outreach/info_activities/pdfs/CTA_the_water_cycle.pdf)*

## 3. Common Compounds & How They Form

All chemical equations balanced. Sources: [LibreTexts Chemistry](https://chem.libretexts.org); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com).

### 3.1 Water (H₂O)
- **Reaction:** 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2 H₂O(g) — *combustion/synthesis reaction*
- **Alternative:** H₂O forms naturally when hydrogen-bearing fuels combust with oxygen
- **Properties:** Colorless liquid; mp 0 °C, bp 100 °C; universal solvent; density 1.0 g/cm³
- **Game recipe:** Hydrogen + Oxygen (+ ignition spark or heat)

### 3.2 Table Salt — Sodium Chloride (NaCl)
- **Reaction:** 2 Na(s) + Cl₂(g) → 2 NaCl(s) — *combination/synthesis reaction*
- **Properties:** White crystalline solid; mp 801 °C; dissolves in water
- **Game recipe:** Sodium + Chlorine
- *Source: [LibreTexts Types of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Valley_City_State_University/Chem_121/Chapter_5:_Introduction_to_Redox_Chemistry/5.3:_Types_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

### 3.3 Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)
- **Reaction (combustion):** C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)
- **Reaction (respiration/fermentation):** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂(g)
- **Properties:** Colorless gas at room temp; sublimes at −78.5 °C (dry ice); denser than air
- **Game recipe:** Carbon + Oxygen (heat); or Fuel combustion; or Fermentation

### 3.4 Rust — Iron(III) Oxide (Fe₂O₃)
- **Reaction:** 4 Fe(s) + 3 O₂(g) → 2 Fe₂O₃(s) — *slow oxidation (corrosion)*
- **Accelerated by:** Water and acid; presence of NaCl speeds the reaction
- **Properties:** Red-brown powder; density ~5.2 g/cm³; magnetic (magnetite variant: Fe₃O₄)
- **Game recipe:** Iron + Oxygen + Water (+ Time)
- *Source: [LibreTexts Description of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Purdue/Purdue_Chem_26100:_Organic_Chemistry_I_(Wenthold)/Chapter_05:_The_Study_of_Chemical_Reactions/5.1_Description_of_Chemical_Reactions/Description_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

### 3.5 Methane (CH₄)
- **Natural formation:** Anaerobic decomposition of organic matter: 2[CH₂O] → CH₄ + CO₂ (fossil fuel formation over millions of years under pressure)
- **Combustion:** CH₄(g) + 2 O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2 H₂O(g) + heat
- **Properties:** Colorless, odorless gas; bp −161.5 °C; highly flammable
- **Game recipe:** Organic Matter + Pressure + Time (geologic)

### 3.6 Ammonia (NH₃)
- **Reaction (Haber Process):** N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g) [high temp ~450 °C, high pressure ~200 atm, Fe catalyst]
- **Properties:** Colorless pungent gas; bp −33.35 °C; dissolves in water to form basic solution
- **Game recipe:** Nitrogen + Hydrogen + Heat + Pressure + Iron catalyst
- *Source: [LibreTexts Description of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Purdue/Purdue_Chem_26100:_Organic_Chemistry_I_(Wenthold)/Chapter_05:_The_Study_of_Chemical_Reactions/5.1_Description_of_Chemical_Reactions/Description_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

### 3.7 Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄)
- **Industrial synthesis (Contact Process):**
  1. S + O₂ → SO₂
  2. 2 SO₂ + O₂ → 2 SO₃ (V₂O₅ catalyst, ~450 °C)
  3. SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
- **Properties:** Colorless oily liquid; bp 337 °C; extremely corrosive; exothermic when diluted
- **Game recipe:** Sulfur + Oxygen → SO₂ + Oxygen → SO₃ + Water → Sulfuric Acid

### 3.8 Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)
- **Synthesis:** H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2 HCl(g) [dissolves in water to form hydrochloric acid]
- **Properties:** Colorless fuming liquid when concentrated; strong acid; bp −85.1 °C (gas)
- **Game recipe:** Hydrogen + Chlorine → HCl gas + Water

### 3.9 Sodium Hydroxide — Lye (NaOH)
- **Synthesis (Chloralkali process):** 2 NaCl + 2 H₂O → 2 NaOH + Cl₂↑ + H₂↑ (electrolysis)
- **Properties:** White solid; mp 318 °C; highly caustic; strongly basic; absorbs CO₂ from air
- **Game recipe:** Salt Water + Electricity (electrolysis)

### 3.10 Glass (Soda-Lime Glass)
- **Synthesis:** SiO₂ (sand) + Na₂CO₃ (soda ash) + CaCO₃ (limestone) → glass + CO₂↑ [melt at ~1,400–1,600 °C, then cool rapidly]
- **Simplified:** Sand + Heat → molten silica → glass
- **Properties:** Amorphous solid; transparent; mp ~1,710 °C (pure silica); density ~2.5 g/cm³
- **Game recipe:** Sand + Heat (very high); or Sand + Soda Ash + Limestone + Heat

### 3.11 Steel
- **Synthesis:** Iron + Carbon (0.2–2.1 wt%) + heat in furnace
  - Wrought iron: <0.08% C (soft, malleable)
  - Steel: 0.2–2.1% C (strong)
  - Cast iron: 2.1–4% C (brittle)
- **Properties:** Stronger and harder than pure iron; density ~7.8 g/cm³; rusts unless protected
- **Game recipe:** Iron + Carbon (+ Heat in furnace)

### 3.12 Bronze
- **Synthesis:** Cu + Sn (typically ~88% copper, 12% tin)
- **Historical significance:** First intentionally produced alloy (~3300 BCE)
- **Properties:** Harder than copper; mp ~950 °C; density ~8.9 g/cm³; corrosion-resistant golden-brown color
- **Game recipe:** Copper + Tin + Heat

### 3.13 Brass
- **Synthesis:** Cu + Zn (typically 60–90% copper, 10–40% zinc)
- **Properties:** Yellow-gold color; harder than copper but softer than steel; mp ~900–940 °C; density ~8.4–8.7 g/cm³
- **Game recipe:** Copper + Zinc + Heat

### 3.14 Concrete
- **Synthesis:**
  1. **Cement production:** CaCO₃ (limestone) + SiO₂ (clay/sand) → heated to ~1,450 °C → clinker (CaO·SiO₂) + CO₂↑
  2. **Concrete:** Cement + Sand + Gravel + Water → hydration reaction → hardened concrete
- **Key reaction:** CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (exothermic); Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (re-carbonation, long-term hardening)
- **Game recipe:** Limestone + Clay + Heat → Cement; then Cement + Sand + Gravel + Water → Concrete

### 3.15 Soap
- **Synthesis (Saponification):** Fat (triglyceride) + NaOH (lye) → Soap (sodium fatty acid salt) + Glycerol
  - Example: C₃H₅(C₁₇H₃₅COO)₃ + 3 NaOH → 3 C₁₇H₃₅COONa + C₃H₅(OH)₃
- **Properties:** Amphiphilic molecules; one end attracts water, other attracts oils/grease
- **Game recipe:** Animal Fat/Oil + Sodium Hydroxide (lye) + Heat

### 3.16 Ethanol — Grain Alcohol (C₂H₅OH)
- **Synthesis (Fermentation):** C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂ [yeast catalyst, anaerobic, ~15–35 °C]
- **Properties:** Colorless liquid; bp 78.3 °C; mp −114.1 °C; miscible with water; flammable
- **Game recipe:** Sugar/Starch + Yeast + Water + Time (fermentation); or Glucose + Yeast
- *Source: [LibreTexts Description of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Purdue/Purdue_Chem_26100:_Organic_Chemistry_I_(Wenthold)/Chapter_05:_The_Study_of_Chemical_Reactions/5.1_Description_of_Chemical_Reactions/Description_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

### 3.17 Vinegar — Acetic Acid (CH₃COOH / HC₂H₃O₂)
- **Synthesis (Acetification):** C₂H₅OH + O₂ → HC₂H₃O₂ + H₂O [Acetobacter bacteria, aerobic]
- **Properties:** Colorless liquid; pungent smell; bp 117.9 °C; mp 16.6 °C (freezes in cold)
- **Game recipe:** Ethanol + Oxygen + Bacteria (Acetobacter) + Time
- *Source: [LibreTexts Description of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Purdue/Purdue_Chem_26100:_Organic_Chemistry_I_(Wenthold)/Chapter_05:_The_Study_of_Chemical_Reactions/5.1_Description_of_Chemical_Reactions/Description_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

## 4. Geology & Earth Materials

*Primary sources: [USGS Collecting Rocks](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/collect1/collectgip.html); [USGS Metamorphic Rocks](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-metamorphic-rocks)*

### 4.1 The Rock Cycle

```
Magma (molten rock)
    ↓ cools (slow = coarse crystals; fast = fine crystals)
IGNEOUS ROCK
    ↓ weathering + erosion → sediment; or heat/pressure
    ↓ sediment + pressure + cementation
SEDIMENTARY ROCK     ← ← ← ← ← ←
    ↓ heat + pressure (metamorphism)          ↑
METAMORPHIC ROCK ─────→ melts → Magma ─────→ 
```

### 4.2 Igneous Rocks

Formed by cooling of molten rock (magma/lava). *Source: [USGS Collecting Rocks](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/collect1/collectgip.html)*

| Rock | Formation | Texture | Key Minerals | Game Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Granite** | Intrusive (slow cooling underground) | Coarse-grained (visible crystals) | Quartz, feldspar, mica | Building stone, countertops |
| **Basalt** | Extrusive (fast cooling from lava) | Fine-grained | Pyroxene, plagioclase | Ocean floor, road base |
| **Obsidian** | Very fast cooling (volcanic glass) | Glassy, no crystals | Silica-rich glass | Cutting tools (prehistoric), ornamental |
| **Pumice** | Lava cooled with gas bubbles trapped | Frothy, porous | Volcanic glass + voids | Abrasive, floating rock (density <1) |
| **Rhyolite** | Extrusive, high silica content | Fine-grained | Quartz, feldspar | Similar composition to granite |

### 4.3 Sedimentary Rocks

Formed by accumulation and lithification of sediment at Earth's surface. *Source: [USGS Collecting Rocks](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/collect1/collectgip.html)*

| Rock | Sediment Source | Key Process | Notable Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sandstone** | Sand-sized quartz particles | Compaction + cementation by silica/calcite | Building material, aquifer rock |
| **Limestone** | Calcium carbonate (marine organisms, chemical precipitation) | Shell accumulation + lithification | Cement making, building stone |
| **Shale** | Clay minerals + fine silt | Compaction under low pressure | Source rock for oil/gas; splits into layers |
| **Conglomerate** | Rounded gravel/pebbles | Cementation | Indicator of ancient river/beach |
| **Coal** | Ancient compressed plant matter (peat) | Anaerobic decomposition → peat → lignite → bituminous coal → anthracite (millions of years + heat + pressure) | Fuel; source of industrial carbon |

### 4.4 Metamorphic Rocks

Formed when existing rocks are transformed by heat and/or pressure without fully melting. *Source: [USGS Metamorphic Rocks FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-metamorphic-rocks)*

| Rock | Parent Rock | Agents | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marble** | Limestone | Heat + pressure | Recrystallized calcite; white/veined; mp CaCO₃ ~825 °C (decomposes) |
| **Slate** | Shale | Low-grade heat + pressure | Cleaves into flat sheets; very fine-grained |
| **Quartzite** | Sandstone | Heat + pressure | Extremely hard; interlocked quartz crystals |
| **Schist** | Shale/basalt | Medium-high grade metamorphism | Shiny mica flakes; foliated |
| **Gneiss** | Granite or other rocks | High-grade metamorphism | Banded light/dark minerals; coarse |

| **Calcite** | CaCO₃ | 3 | Fizzes with acid (CO₂); dissolves in acid | Limestone, marble |
| **Pyrite** | FeS₂ | 6–6.5 | Fool's gold; metallic luster; sulfide ore | Sedimentary, igneous rocks |
| **Magnetite** | Fe₃O₄ | 5.5–6 | Magnetic; black; iron ore | Igneous and metamorphic rocks |
| **Hematite** | Fe₂O₃ | 5–6 | Red streak; iron ore | Iron formations, sedimentary |

### 4.6 Soil, Clay, Sand, Gravel

| Material | Composition | Formation | Game Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sand** | Mostly quartz (SiO₂) grains | Weathering of rocks + erosion | Glass, concrete, building |
| **Gravel** | Mixed rock fragments >2 mm | Erosion of bedrock | Concrete, drainage |
| **Clay** | Fine aluminosilicate minerals (kaolinite, montmorillonite) | Chemical weathering of feldspar | Ceramics, pottery, brick, absorbs water |
| **Soil** | Clay + sand + organic matter (humus) + microorganisms | Weathering + biological activity | Agriculture, filtration |

### 4.7 Fossil Fuels Formation

| Fuel | Original Material | Process | Timescale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peat → Coal** | Ancient land plants (swamp forests) | Anaerobic decay → peat → burial → increasing heat/pressure → lignite → bituminous coal → anthracite | 300–400 million years |
| **Petroleum (crude oil)** | Marine microorganisms (algae, zooplankton) | Organic matter buried under sediment → anoxic conditions → heat + pressure converts kerogen to oil | ~50–300 million years |
| **Natural Gas (methane)** | Same as oil, or deeper burial/higher temperature | Same process at greater depth/temperature; also produced by anaerobic decomposition of organic matter | ~50–300 million years |

*Source: [LibreTexts Carbon Cycle](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Portland_Community_College/CH100:_Everyday_Chemistry/07:_Climate_and_the_Environment/7.03:_The_Carbon_Cycle)*

### 4.8 Diamond Formation

- **Natural process:** Carbon subjected to ~45–60 kilobars pressure and ~900–1,300 °C temperature, ~145–200 km deep in Earth's mantle, over millions of years
- **Reaction:** C (graphite) + extreme pressure + heat → C (diamond lattice)
- **Brought to surface:** Kimberlite volcanic eruptions (deep-source volcanism)
- **Synthetic (industrial):** High-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) process replicates natural conditions in lab
- **Properties:** Hardest natural substance (Mohs 10); density 3.51 g/cm³ (vs graphite 2.2 g/cm³); sublimes at ~3,825 °C
- *Source: [RSC Periodic Table — Carbon](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/6/carbon)*

## 5. Water Cycle & Weather

*Primary sources: [NOAA NWRFC Water Cycle](https://www.nwrfc.noaa.gov/info/water_cycle/hydrology.html); [NOAA Ocean Today Water Cycle](https://oceantoday.noaa.gov/watercycle/); [NOAA NESDIS Water Cycle Lesson](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2025-08/WaterCycleActivity-Manual.pdf)*

### 5.1 The Water Cycle — Causal Chain

```
SOLAR ENERGY heats surface water
    ↓ EVAPORATION (liquid → water vapor)
    ~600 cal/g of energy absorbed
    ↓ Water vapor rises into atmosphere
    ↓ CONDENSATION (water vapor cools → liquid droplets)
    ↓ Forms CLOUDS (tiny droplets around dust/sea-salt nuclei)
    ↓ Droplets collide and grow → PRECIPITATION
    ↓ Falls as RAIN (>0 °C) or SNOW (<0 °C) or HAIL (ice pellets) or SLEET
    ↓ RUNOFF → rivers → oceans; or INFILTRATION → groundwater
    ↓ TRANSPIRATION from plants returns water vapor to atmosphere
    ↓ (cycle repeats)
```

### 5.2 Precipitation Forms — Conditions

| Form | Condition | Formation Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Rain** | Air temp > 0 °C throughout | Water droplets coalesce and fall |
| **Snow** | Air temp ≤ 0 °C | Ice crystals form in clouds and fall as snowflakes |
| **Hail** | Strong updrafts in cumulonimbus clouds | Ice pellets lofted repeatedly, growing layers of ice (4–40 mm) |
| **Sleet** | Snow falls through warm layer then refreezes | Snow → partially melts → refreezes before impact |
| **Freezing Rain** | Snow melts, then falls on <0 °C surface | Liquid rain freezes on contact with ground |
| **Fog** | Ground-level cloud; air at dewpoint | Water vapor condenses on particles near ground; dewpoint = air temp |

### 5.3 Cloud Types (Altitude-Based)

| Cloud | Altitude | Weather Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Cirrus | >6,000 m | Fair but weather change possible; ice crystals |
| Altocumulus | 2,000–6,000 m | Unsettled weather |
| Stratus | <2,000 m | Overcast; drizzle possible |
| Cumulus | 500–2,000 m (base) | Fair weather; puffy |
| Cumulonimbus | 500 m to 12,000 m | Thunderstorms, heavy rain, hail, tornadoes |
| Nimbostratus | 2,000–4,000 m | Continuous rain or snow |

### 5.4 Lightning & Thunder

- **Formation:** In cumulonimbus clouds, ice particles and water droplets separate electrically (negatively charged bases, positively charged tops)
- **Strike:** Electrical discharge between cloud base and ground (or cloud-to-cloud) neutralizes charge difference
- **Temperature:** ~30,000 K (five times hotter than surface of the sun) — creates plasma channel
- **Thunder:** Rapid heating of air creates shockwave → sound wave
- **Game recipe:** Storm Cloud + Electrical Charge Separation → Lightning → Thunder

### 5.5 Rainbow

- **Formation:** Sunlight refracted and internally reflected inside raindrops; light disperses into spectrum by wavelength
- **Condition:** Sun behind observer, rain in front; sun angle <42°
- **Real science:** Red (42°), orange, yellow, green, blue, violet (40°) — each color refracted at slightly different angle
- **Game recipe:** Sunlight + Rain (or Water Droplets in Air) + Specific Angle

### 5.6 Ice, Snow, and Frost

| Phenomenon | Phase Change | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Ice** | Liquid water → solid (freezing) | Temperature drops to/below 0 °C |
| **Frost** | Water vapor → solid (deposition) | Surface temperature <0 °C; humidity sufficient |
| **Snow** | Liquid droplets → ice crystals in cloud | Cloud temp <0 °C; grows into hexagonal snowflake |
| **Glacier** | Accumulated snow → recrystallized ice | Decades+ of snow accumulation + compression |

## 6. Basic Physics Processes

### 6.1 Combustion

**Complete combustion:** Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Heat + Light  
**Incomplete combustion (insufficient O₂):** Fuel + O₂ → CO + H₂O + soot (carbon particles) + Heat

| Fuel | Combustion Equation | Heat of Combustion |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen | 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O | −286 kJ/mol |
| Methane (natural gas) | CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O | −890 kJ/mol |
| Carbon | C + O₂ → CO₂ | −394 kJ/mol |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH + 3 O₂ → 2 CO₂ + 3 H₂O | −1,367 kJ/mol |

**Fire triangle:** Requires 3 elements — Fuel + Heat (ignition temperature) + Oxygen. Remove any one to extinguish.  
**Game recipe:** Fuel + Oxygen + Heat → Fire + CO₂ + Water vapor

*Source: [LibreTexts Types of Chemical Reactions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Valley_City_State_University/Chem_121/Chapter_5:_Introduction_to_Redox_Chemistry/5.3:_Types_of_Chemical_Reactions)*

### 6.2 Electricity

**Generation methods:**

| Method | Process | Key Ingredient |
|---|---|---|
| **Electromagnetic induction** | Moving conductor through magnetic field (generator) | Motion + Magnet + Conductor (copper wire) |
| **Chemical (battery)** | Redox reaction: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu | Two metals + electrolyte solution |
| **Photoelectric (solar)** | Photons knock electrons from silicon lattice | Sunlight + semiconductor (Si) |
| **Thermoelectric** | Temperature gradient across junction of two metals | Heat differential |
| **Friction (static)** | Electrons transferred between surfaces | Two different materials rubbing together |

**Key components of a circuit:** Voltage source (battery/generator) + Conductor (copper) + Load (resistor/bulb) + Switch  
**Conductors vs. Insulators:**
- Best conductors: Silver, copper, gold, aluminum
- Semiconductors: Silicon, germanium (current control)
- Insulators: Rubber, glass, ceramic, plastic

### 6.3 Magnetism

- **Ferromagnetic materials** (can be magnetized): Iron, Nickel, Cobalt, Neodymium (Nd)
- **Magnetic field:** Flows from N pole to S pole outside magnet; from S to N inside
- **Electromagnet:** Current through coil of wire wrapped around iron core → temporary magnet
- **Permanent magnet:** Neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) — strongest common permanent magnet
- **Relationship to electricity:** Changing magnetic field induces electric current (Faraday's Law); electric current creates magnetic field (Ampere's Law)
- **Game recipe:** Iron + Electricity/Coil → Electromagnet; Magnetite (Fe₃O₄) → Natural Magnet

### 6.4 Light (Electromagnetic Radiation)

| Type | Wavelength Range | Energy | Game Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radio waves | >1 mm | Lowest | Radio communication |
| Infrared (heat radiation) | 700 nm – 1 mm | Medium-low | Heat transfer; night vision |
| Visible light | 380–700 nm | Medium | Vision; photosynthesis; rainbows |
| Ultraviolet (UV) | 10–380 nm | Medium-high | Causes sunburn; kills bacteria |
| X-rays | 0.01–10 nm | High | Medical imaging; ionizing |
| Gamma rays | <0.01 nm | Highest | Nuclear decay; sterilization |

**Speed of light:** 299,792,458 m/s (in vacuum)  
**Light sources in game context:** Fire, incandescence (hot objects glow), fluorescence (excited electrons), bioluminescence (chemical energy → light in living things)

### 6.5 Heat Transfer

| Mechanism | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | Heat passes through solid material particle-to-particle | Metal pan on stove heats up |
| **Convection** | Hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks (circulation) | Boiling water, atmospheric weather cells |
| **Radiation** | Electromagnetic waves carry energy through vacuum | Sun heating Earth; fire warming you |

**Specific heat capacity** (energy needed to raise 1 g by 1 °C):
- Water: 4.18 J/(g·°C) — very high; great heat buffer
- Iron: 0.45 J/(g·°C)
- Copper: 0.39 J/(g·°C)
- Air: ~1.0 J/(g·°C)

### 6.6 Pressure

- **Definition:** Force per unit area (Pa = N/m²)
- **Atmospheric pressure:** 101,325 Pa = 1 atm at sea level
- **Pressure increases melting points** of most substances (raising the phase boundary)
- **Pressure-temperature effects in game:**
  - Diamond requires ~45,000+ atm (4.5 GPa) to form from graphite
  - Deep ocean: ~600 atm at 6,000 m depth
  - High pressure lowers boiling point (water boils at 71 °C on top of Everest at 0.34 atm)
  - High pressure raises boiling point (pressure cooker: water at 121 °C at 2 atm)

### 6.7 Density & Buoyancy

**Density** = mass/volume (g/cm³ or kg/m³)

| Material | Density (g/cm³) |
|---|---|
| Helium gas | 0.000164 |
| Air | 0.00120 |
| Ice | 0.917 |
| Water (4°C) | 1.000 |
| Seawater | 1.025 |
| Concrete | ~2.3 |
| Granite | ~2.7 |
| Aluminum | 2.70 |
| Silicon | 2.33 |
| Iron | 7.87 |
| Copper | 8.96 |
| Lead | 11.3 |
| Mercury | 13.53 |
| Gold | 19.3 |

**Archimedes' Principle:** Object floats if its average density < fluid density.  
Ice floats on water (density 0.917 < 1.000) — crucial for life (lakes freeze from top, not bottom).

### 6.8 Dissolving & Solutions

- **Solute:** Substance being dissolved (e.g., salt)
- **Solvent:** Dissolving medium (e.g., water)
- **"Like dissolves like":** Polar solvents (water) dissolve polar/ionic substances; nonpolar solvents dissolve nonpolar substances
- **Solubility factors:** Temperature (usually ↑ solubility with heat for solids; ↓ for gases), pressure (increases gas solubility)
- **Key dissolutions in game:**
  - NaCl in water → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ ions (conducting solution)
  - CO₂ in water → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid; acidifies solution)
  - HCl in water → H⁺ + Cl⁻ (hydrochloric acid — very acidic)
  - NaOH in water → Na⁺ + OH⁻ (very basic/alkaline, exothermic)

### 6.9 Friction

- **Static friction:** Prevents motion between surfaces at rest
- **Kinetic friction:** Opposes motion between sliding surfaces
- **Frictional heat:** Can ignite materials (matches, fire-starting by friction: wood on wood reaches ~250–300 °C to ignite)
- **Game recipe:** Flint + Iron Pyrite (striking friction) → Spark → Ignition; Wood + Wood (friction) → Ember

## 7. Simple Biology & Origin of Life Chain

*Sources: [Britannica Miller-Urey experiment](https://www.britannica.com/science/Miller-Urey-experiment); [Britannica Oparin-Haldane theory](https://www.britannica.com/science/Oparin-Haldane-theory); [Britannica Photosynthesis](https://www.britannica.com/science/photosynthesis)*

### 7.1 Origin of Life — Scientific Grounding

The accepted scientific framework (Oparin-Haldane hypothesis, confirmed by Miller-Urey 1953 and subsequent work):

1. **Early Earth atmosphere** (~4 billion years ago): Reducing atmosphere — H₂O vapor, CH₄, NH₃, H₂, CO₂; very little free O₂
2. **Energy sources:** UV radiation, lightning, volcanic heat, hydrothermal vents
3. **Abiotic synthesis:** Simple inorganic molecules react to form **amino acids and nucleotides** (building blocks)
4. **Concentration:** In tidal pools or hydrothermal vents, molecules concentrate (wet-dry cycles)
5. **Polymerization:** Amino acids link into proteins; nucleotides link into RNA/DNA
6. **Self-replication:** RNA molecules capable of catalysis and self-copying emerge
7. **Protocells:** Fatty acid membranes spontaneously form vesicles (proto-cell membranes) enclosing replicating molecules
8. **First cells:** Prokaryotes (~3.5–3.8 billion years ago) — no nucleus, simple membrane

*Source: [Miller-Urey experiment, Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/Miller-Urey-experiment); [Oparin-Haldane theory, Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/Oparin-Haldane-theory)*

### 7.2 Biological Progression Chain (Game Tier)

```
INORGANIC CHEMISTRY
    ↓ H₂O + CH₄ + NH₃ + H₂ + CO₂ + Energy (lightning/UV/heat)
PRIMORDIAL SOUP / ORGANIC MOLECULES
    ↓ Amino acids, nucleotides, simple sugars
PROTEINS + RNA
    ↓ Self-replicating RNA; fatty acid vesicles
PROTOCELL
    ↓ Lipid membrane + nucleic acid + basic metabolism
FIRST BACTERIA (Prokaryote)
    ~3.5 billion years ago; anaerobic; no nucleus
    ↓ Mutation + natural selection
CYANOBACTERIA (Blue-green algae)
    ~2.7 billion years ago; first PHOTOSYNTHESIS
    → Produces O₂ → Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 Ga)
    → Transforms atmosphere from reducing to oxidizing
    ↓ 
ALGAE (Simple eukaryotes; nucleus)
    ~1.5 billion years ago
    ↓
PLANTS (Multicellular, photosynthetic)
    ~500 million years ago (land plants); complex tissues
    ↓
FUNGI
    ~1.4 billion years ago (evolved independently); decomposers
    ↓
SIMPLE ANIMALS (Cambrian explosion ~540 million years ago)
    Invertebrates → fish → amphibians → reptiles → mammals
```

### 7.3 Photosynthesis (Key Reaction)

**Overall balanced equation:**

```
6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + 6 O₂
```

- **Light reactions** (in thylakoid membrane): 2H₂O + light → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻
- **Dark reactions / Calvin Cycle** (in stroma): CO₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → CH₂O + H₂O
- **Energy:** ΔG° = +479 kJ/mol (endergonic; requires sunlight)
- **Relevance:** Produces all atmospheric oxygen; basis of all food chains; fixes carbon from air into biomass

*Source: [Britannica Photosynthesis](https://www.britannica.com/science/photosynthesis); [PMC Photosynthesis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5264509/)*

### 7.4 Respiration (Reverse of Photosynthesis)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + 2,870 kJ/mol (ATP energy)
```

**Game connection:** Fire is rapid oxidation (combustion); respiration is controlled slow oxidation of the same substrates.

## 8. Proposed Realistic Progression Tree

This tree organizes ~8–12 tiers from primitive "classical elements" to technology. Every arrow represents a **real process**.

### Tier 0 — Primordial Bases (Starting Elements)
```
EARTH   WATER   FIRE   AIR (atmosphere)
  + ENERGY/HEAT   + PRESSURE   + TIME
```

### Tier 1 — Fundamental Elements
```
EARTH → [grinding/erosion] → SAND, CLAY, ROCK
WATER + AIR → [evaporation] → WATER VAPOR, CLOUDS
FIRE → [plasma] → LIGHTNING, PLASMA
AIR → [fractionation] → NITROGEN (78%), OXYGEN (21%), ARGON (1%)
WATER → [electrolysis] → HYDROGEN + OXYGEN
ROCK + PRESSURE + HEAT → [metamorphism] → METAMORPHIC ROCK
```

### Tier 2 — Early Chemistry
```
HYDROGEN + OXYGEN + IGNITION → WATER (synthesis)
NITROGEN + HYDROGEN + HEAT + PRESSURE → AMMONIA (Haber process)
CARBON + OXYGEN → CO₂ (combustion)
SAND + HEAT (>1,710 °C) → MOLTEN SILICA → GLASS
SODIUM + CHLORINE → TABLE SALT
SULFUR + OXYGEN → SULFUR DIOXIDE
SULFUR DIOXIDE + OXYGEN → SULFUR TRIOXIDE
SULFUR TRIOXIDE + WATER → SULFURIC ACID
```

### Tier 3 — Geology & Earth Processes
```
MAGMA [slow cooling] → GRANITE
MAGMA [fast cooling] → BASALT
BASALT + WEATHERING → CLAY + SAND
SAND + PRESSURE + CEMENTATION → SANDSTONE
LIMESTONE + PRESSURE + HEAT → MARBLE
IRON ORE (hematite/magnetite) → [found in metamorphic/igneous rock]
COAL → [ancient plants + pressure + time]
OIL → [ancient organisms + pressure + time + depth]
CARBON + PRESSURE (45,000 atm) + HEAT (900–1,300 °C) → DIAMOND
```

### Tier 4 — Metals & Alloys (Early Technology)
```
IRON ORE + HEAT (>1,538 °C) → IRON [smelting]
IRON + CARBON (0.2–2.1%) + HEAT → STEEL
COPPER ORE + HEAT (>1,084 °C) → COPPER
COPPER + TIN + HEAT → BRONZE [first alloy, Bronze Age]
COPPER + ZINC + HEAT → BRASS
GOLD [found native; mp 1,064 °C] → GOLD METAL
TIN ORE + HEAT → TIN
LEAD ORE + HEAT → LEAD
```

### Tier 5 — Chemical Industry
```
IRON + OXYGEN + WATER + TIME → RUST (Fe₂O₃)
ANIMAL FAT + SODIUM HYDROXIDE → SOAP (saponification)
LIMESTONE + CLAY + HEAT → CEMENT
CEMENT + SAND + GRAVEL + WATER → CONCRETE
SUGAR/STARCH + YEAST → ETHANOL + CO₂ (fermentation)
ETHANOL + OXYGEN + BACTERIA → ACETIC ACID (vinegar)
HYDROGEN CHLORIDE + WATER → HYDROCHLORIC ACID
SALT WATER + ELECTRICITY → SODIUM HYDROXIDE + CHLORINE + HYDROGEN
```

### Tier 6 — Earth Atmosphere & Weather
```
WATER + HEAT (sun) → WATER VAPOR [evaporation]
WATER VAPOR + COOLING → CLOUD [condensation around particles]
CLOUD + TEMPERATURE ≤ 0°C → SNOW
CLOUD + FREEZING LAYER → HAIL
CUMULONIMBUS + CHARGE SEPARATION → LIGHTNING + THUNDER
SUNLIGHT + RAINDROPS + CORRECT ANGLE → RAINBOW
WATER VAPOR + SURFACE <0°C → FROST [deposition]
```

### Tier 7 — Origin of Life
```
CO₂ + H₂O + CH₄ + NH₃ + LIGHTNING/UV → AMINO ACIDS [primordial soup, Miller-Urey]
AMINO ACIDS + NUCLEOTIDES + WET-DRY CYCLES → RNA [self-replication]
RNA + FATTY ACIDS → PROTOCELL
PROTOCELL + TIME + MUTATION → BACTERIUM (prokaryote)
BACTERIUM + CO₂ + H₂O + LIGHT → CYANOBACTERIUM [photosynthesis evolves]
CYANOBACTERIUM + TIME → O₂ ATMOSPHERE [Great Oxidation Event]
O₂ + UV LIGHT → OZONE (O₃) [stratospheric; protects surface life]
```

### Tier 8 — Complex Life
```
CYANOBACTERIUM + ENDOSYMBIOSIS → ALGAE (first eukaryote)
ALGAE + TIME → PLANT (multicellular; photosynthesis on land)
SPORES/SPORES → FUNGI (decomposers)
PLANT MATTER + FUNGI + BACTERIA → SOIL
SOIL + WATER + SUNLIGHT + PLANTS → ECOSYSTEM
MARINE ORGANISMS + EVOLUTIONARY TIME → SIMPLE ANIMALS
```

### Tier 9 — Advanced Materials & Technology
```
SILICON (purified from sand/silica) → SEMICONDUCTOR
COPPER + ELECTRICITY → ELECTROMAGNET
IRON + COIL + MOTION → ELECTRIC GENERATOR (Faraday's Law)
COAL/OIL + COMBUSTION → STEAM PRESSURE → STEAM ENGINE
STEAM ENGINE + IRON → INDUSTRIAL MACHINE
ALUMINUM OXIDE (bauxite) + ELECTROLYSIS → ALUMINUM METAL
SILICON + BORON/PHOSPHORUS DOPING → TRANSISTOR
TRANSISTOR + COPPER WIRES + SILICON → COMPUTER CHIP
```

### Tier 10 — Nuclear (End-Game)
```
URANIUM ORE → ENRICHED URANIUM (²³⁵U)
²³⁵U + NEUTRON → FISSION → KRYPTON + BARIUM + 3 NEUTRONS + ENERGY
FISSION + CONTROLLED CHAIN REACTION → NUCLEAR REACTOR → ELECTRICITY
HYDROGEN + PRESSURE + HEAT (>10⁸ °C) → FUSION → HELIUM + ENERGY (solar process)
```
